# Why AI Adoption Stalls in SMEs | A Realistic Roadmap Starting from General Affairs and Administration

**For SMEs, AI adoption should begin with "daily document-related tasks" rather than flashy automation**

AI adoption among small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) is not as far along as news reports might suggest. While adoption rates are rising, many companies face roadblocks such as "not knowing what to use it for," "a lack of talent," and "concerns about whether it is safe to input information." That is precisely why a realistic first step is not a large-scale AI system, but rather the document workflows in general affairs and administrative departments.

## Why AI Adoption Often Stalls in SMEs

The reason is not simply that the technology is difficult. Rather, it is largely because it remains undecided which tasks to start with, who will take ownership, and what information may be fed into the AI.

Treating AI adoption as a "company-wide transformation" from the outset makes it unwieldy. Initially, you should narrow your focus to workflows where saved time is easy to see and information handling is straightforward to organize.

## Why Start with General Affairs and Administration?

General affairs and administrative departments hold a concentration of document-heavy workflows that are highly compatible with AI: drafting meeting minutes, searching internal regulations, preliminary review of contracts, answering inquiries, verifying expenses and billing, and maintaining operational manuals.

While these impose a heavy burden on front-line staff, their outcomes are easy to measure. You can quantify the results after introducing AI: how many minutes were shaved off creating meeting minutes, how many fewer internal inquiries were made, or how much faster the initial review of contracts became.

## 90-Day Roadmap

- Weeks 1–2: Decide on the target tasks, what information may be inputted, and which AI tools to use.
- Weeks 3–4: Pilot with a single workflow, such as meeting minutes, internal FAQs, or preliminary contract reviews.
- Weeks 5–8: Gather usage logs, time saved, and adjustments needed on the front lines.
- Weeks 9–12: Differentiate between tasks suitable for cloud AI and those that warrant consideration of local LLMs or isolated environments.

Following this sequence allows you to build the decision-making criteria for AI adoption without unnecessarily purchasing a massive system.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which tasks should SMEs start using AI for?

It is practical to start with tasks that involve large volumes of documents and clear, measurable results, such as drafting meeting minutes, internal FAQs, preliminary review of contracts, organizing quotes, and responding to inquiries.

### Is specialized talent required for AI adoption?

It is not required for the initial validation phase. However, when you reach the stage of handling confidential information or integrating with internal systems, design involving external experts becomes necessary.

### How do you distinguish between using cloud AI and local LLMs?

Consider cloud AI for public information and general documents, and local LLMs or approved environments for customer confidential data, personal information, internal proprietary documents, and similar materials.
