## Bad PoCs Only Look at "Whether the Technology Works"

A common failure mode in AI PoCs is evaluating only model accuracy, UI appearance, and the impact of the demo. Technical validation is necessary, of course. However, that alone does not provide the basis for deciding on production deployment.

What you really need to examine is how much business hours are reduced, whose decision-making is made easier, what data is required, who assumes operational responsibility, and whether there are security or access permission issues.

## KPIs to Evaluate at 30 Days

- Utilization rate: How many times target users actually used it.
- Accuracy/correction rate: How much of the AI output required human edits.
- Time saved: How many minutes were saved compared to conventional workflows.
- Exception patterns: What cases the AI struggles with.
- User feedback: Reasons why users want to continue using it, or why they do not use it.

At the 30-day mark, rather than demanding flawless ROI, the goal is to determine whether the workflow warrants continued validation.

## KPIs to Evaluate at 90 Days

- Production target workflows: Which specific workflows will be targeted for adoption.
- Cost-effectiveness: Whether time savings, quality improvements, inquiry reductions, revenue contributions, etc., can be articulated in figures.
- Operating structure: Who owns data updates, permissions, logging, user support, and improvement cycles.
- Risk management: Whether rules exist for incorrect answers, data leaks, rights, and customer-facing explanations.
- Next-phase investment: How much investment will be needed after the PoC, and what specific outcomes will be targeted.

If a PoC cannot enable these decisions at 90 days, its initial design was flawed. Adopting AI is not about "building something that works," but about "building the justification to continue."

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q. What should be decided first in an AI PoC?

The target workflow, success KPIs, failure criteria, data scope, operational owner, and the deadline for deciding on production deployment.

### Q. Why do PoCs tend to stall?

Because even if they work as technical demos, business KPIs, cost-effectiveness, permissions, and operational structures have not been decided, preventing the project from moving forward to an executive decision.

### Q. Can a production deployment decision be made in 30 days?

In most cases, 30 days is used not to make the final production decision, but to determine whether the initiative warrants continued validation. A realistic timeframe for evaluating production deployment is around 90 days.
