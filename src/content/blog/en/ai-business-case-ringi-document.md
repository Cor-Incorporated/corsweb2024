## The 6 Essential Elements for Approval Documentation

- Purpose: Which operational challenge are you solving?
- Scope: Who will use it, and in which workflows?
- KPIs: What will be measured—hours saved, quality improvements, reduced inquiries, or revenue contribution?
- Costs: Separate initial costs, monthly fees, operational expenses, and training costs.
- Risks: Address information leaks, inaccurate outputs, permissions, legal compliance, and operational accountability.
- Next Decision: How will you decide whether to continue, halt, or expand after 30 and 90 days?

An approval proposal lacking these six elements is merely an explanatory document about AI, not an investment decision document.

## Do Not View Cost-Effectiveness Solely Through "Hours Saved"

The benefits of adopting AI extend beyond just reducing working hours. Proposal speed, decision quality, eliminating dependency on specific individuals, transparency in client explanations, and knowledge accumulation are also crucial.

In quotation workflows, for example, it is not merely about shortening preparation time; being able to provide justification based on past projects and market rates elevates the quality of business negotiations. This is also vital for avoiding price competition.

## Proposals That Omit Risks Are Weak

The more an employee wants to push AI adoption through, the more reluctant they are to write down risks. However, decision-makers are most likely to reject proposals where risks are invisible. Information management, inaccurate outputs, permissions, logging, client explanations, and internal training should be detailed upfront.

Show how you will manage risks rather than hiding them. This is what matters in an approval proposal for AI adoption. Cor.'s security policy also reflects this approach: operating by combining confidentiality tiers, approved AI tools, minimal security logging, and an incident response framework.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q. What should be written first in an approval request for AI adoption?

Not AI capabilities, but the operational challenge to solve, scope, KPIs, costs, risks, and decision deadlines.

### Q. How should cost-effectiveness be demonstrated?

Organize KPIs to capture not only hours saved, but also proposal speed, quality improvements, eliminating dependency on specific individuals, reduced inquiries, and transparency in client explanations.

### Q. What is required to justify quote figures?

You need documentation that organizes functional requirements, man-hour breakdowns, similar projects, market rates, team track records, and risk factors to explain why it costs that specific amount.
